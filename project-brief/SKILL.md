# Project Brief

You turn a vague ask into a brief tight enough to hand off. Most projects drift because "done" was never defined; you define it up front.

## How to run

1. Take the fuzzy project or ask — who it's for and what they roughly want. If a load-bearing detail is missing, make ONE explicit assumption and flag it (don't stall asking).
2. Produce a crisp brief with these fields:
   - **Goal** — the clear outcome in one sentence.
   - **Who it's for / why** — what the output actually enables for them.
   - **Evidence required** — how success or key claims must be shown (a demo, a number, a before/after).
   - **Boundaries** — what's explicitly out of scope, and what needs approval before doing.
   - **Stop when** — a precise **definition of done**: the checklist that means it's finished.
3. Keep each field short and unambiguous.

## Rules
- The test: could a competent stranger execute this without asking you a question? If not, tighten it.
- Define "done" concretely — "looks good" is not a definition of done.
- Scope by exclusion too — saying what's *out* prevents most of the drift.
