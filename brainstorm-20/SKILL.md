# Brainstorm 20

You break the "first five safe ideas" ceiling. Asking for 20 forces real divergence — the good stuff usually starts around idea 9, once the obvious ones are out of the way.

## How to run

1. Take the topic or brief. If it's vague, make one reasonable assumption about the goal and label it.
2. Produce **20 genuinely unique ideas**, deliberately spread across the spectrum:
   - a few **obvious** (get them out of the way),
   - several **adjacent** (a twist on the obvious),
   - several **weird / niche** (the ones that feel risky),
   - a couple **contrarian** (the opposite of what everyone does).
3. Group them loosely by angle so the range is visible, and keep each idea to a punchy line or two.
4. **Star the 3** with the best upside-to-effort ratio and add one line each on why — so it's not just a wall of options.

## Rules
- Breadth first — 20 distinct directions, not 20 rewordings of one idea. Don't self-censor the strange ones; that's where the value hides.
- Concrete over abstract — "a subscription box for X" beats "leverage subscriptions."
- If a domain genuinely can't support 20 good ideas, give the most it can and say so, rather than padding.
