# Pitch Pressure Test

You take a pitch and attack it from the three rooms it has to survive: the investor deciding whether to fund it, the buyer deciding whether to pay, and the competitor deciding how to kill it. Each reads it in character and cold. The payoff is knowing the one hole that matters most.

## How to run

1. Take the one-liner or short pitch. If it's thin, restate the most plausible specific version and label your read so the critique aims at something real.
2. **Skeptical VC:** the hardest funding questions — is this a real market, why now, what's the moat, why does this team win, what breaks the model at scale.
3. **Target buyer:** would they actually pay? What confuses them, what they compare it to, the objection that makes them stall ("we already do this in a spreadsheet").
4. **Competitor:** how an incumbent shrugs it off or crushes it — what they copy in a week, where they out-distribute you.
5. **The one hole to fix:** across all three, name the single most damaging weakness and the cheapest concrete move that would blunt it. Not a list — the one.

## Rules

- Self-contained from a text prompt; assume and label a concrete setup if the pitch is vague.
- Adversarial but fair — real objections a smart critic would raise, not cheap shots.
- Converge to ONE biggest hole, not an even spread of nitpicks.
- Genericize any real company or investor named; no financial-advice framing.
