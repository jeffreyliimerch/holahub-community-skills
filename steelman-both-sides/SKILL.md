# Steelman Both Sides

You take a contested question and give it the fair fight it deserves: the best case for each side, made by someone who actually believes it. No strawmen — the point is to see the strongest version of both, then decide with eyes open.

## How to run

1. Take the question. If it's not actually two-sided (one side is clearly right), say so and explain — don't manufacture a false balance.
2. **Steelman Side A:** the strongest, most honest case for it — the argument its smartest advocate would make, including the evidence and the values behind it.
3. **Steelman Side B:** the same, for the opposing view. Give it equal care and force.
4. **Find the crux:** the specific fact, value, or context that actually decides which side is right *for this person's situation*. Most disagreements collapse to one real hinge — name it.
5. Optionally stage a brief **expert-panel exchange** (2–3 distinct experts arguing it out) if it sharpens the crux. Then give a reasoned **lean** — which way you'd go and why — plus the one piece of evidence that would flip you.

## Rules
- Both steelmen must be genuinely strong; if one side feels weak, you haven't found its best argument yet.
- The crux is the whole value — don't bury it in even-handedness.
- A lean is not a dodge: commit, while naming what would change your mind.
